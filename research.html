Research

I investigate properties of complex matter within the framework of statistical and condensed matter physics. I enjoy searching for avenues that translate fundamental principles into modern technological applications. Broadly, my present research interests are in quantum sensing with cavity quantum materials [can we mine the quantum resources within quantum materials by coupling them to quantum light?], machine learning [a grand manifestation of classical (and sometimes quantum) complex systems], quantum criticality [foundational organizing principle; generates some of the most unusual behaviors of matter], and topological materials [a frontier for correlation physics and non-equilibrium phenomena].

My work can be divided into 3 phases based on its primary focus.

  • Phase I: I started my research career devising variational algorithms based on the projected entangled paired states (PEPS; a type of tensor network states above one dimension) as part of my master thesis. My doctoral work established several milestones in theoretical modeling of non-Fermi liquid states, including the only exactly solved scaling theory of a two dimensional non-Fermi liquid without randomness. Over the next three years I studied other forms of anomalous quantum liquids, especially in spin (i.e. magnetic) and pure bosonic systems. Alongside, I also developed renormalization group schemes for semi-metals and investigated metals with highly constrained electronic dynamics.

  • Phase II: Inspired by the developments in topological band theory and its implications for many-body physics, in the next phase I focused on actual quantum materials. The motivation was to determine how the theoretical frameworks developed in Phase I inform ongoing experiments. During this time I worked on developing first-principles-friendly diagnostics of topological structures that remain hidden within Kramer’s degenerate subspaces, and connecting lattice symmetries with constraints they place on electronic motion and strong correlation phenomena. The resultant works dominate the publication category below titled Topological Materials. In parallel, I was drawn to the many facets of non-equilibrium phenomena in topological and strongly correlated systems. This led to a breakthrough in the field of strange metals where we successfully demonstrated how the current shot-noise can serve as a probe of strong correlations.

  • Phase III: In the latest phase, I aim to push the insights gained during phases I & II towards the frontier of technology. My work has two branches. On the first branch sits the question: how can we utilize the enormous quantum resources that are intrinsic to quantum materials to build new types of quantum machines, such as sensors and batteries. My works in this direction are listed below under Quantum Resource Engineering. Concurrently, I have become interested in a complex systems perspective of the principles and mechanisms underlying machine learning. I apply tools from statistical physics and dynamical systems to develop controlled minimal models that can be thoroughly understood in the spirit of the models of complex systems in physics.

The broad topics that I have worked on are summarized by the TOC on the right with publications details below.
